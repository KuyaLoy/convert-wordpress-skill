Three things to know first

  • When you save, the change is on the live website straight away.
  • You change the words and photos. The design stays the same, so you cannot break the look of the site.
  • Not sure about a change? Ask us first. We are happy to help.

1. Logging in

  1. Go to {{DOMAIN}}/wp-admin in your web browser. Save it as a bookmark.
  2. Type your username and password, then click Log In.

Your login is your own. We sent it to you separately. Please do not share it. If someone else needs to edit the website, ask us and we will make them their own login.

Forgot your password?

  1. On the login page, click Lost your password?
  2. Type your username or email address and click Get New Password.
  3. Open the email we send you and click the link. Choose a new password and save it.

No email after 10 minutes? Check your junk folder, then ask us.

3. Editing a page

  1. Click Pages in the menu, then click the name of the page.
  2. The page opens in boxes, one for each part of the page, in the same order as the page itself (for example the top banner, then the services, then the questions).
  3. Click into a box and change the words.
  4. Click [[the save button's label: Save or Update]] at the top right.
  5. Click View Page to check your change on the website.

Text boxes

Text boxes have two buttons: B makes words bold and the link button adds a link. The design looks after everything else. A short note above each box says what it is for.

Lists (questions, steps, cards)

  • To add an item, click the Add button under the list.
  • To move an item, drag it by its number.
  • To remove an item, point at it and click the minus sign on its right.
  • An empty list is simply not shown on the website.

4. Phone, email and address: change them once

Details that appear on many pages are kept in one place. Change them there, and every page shows the new detail.

  1. Click Theme Settings in the menu.
  2. Click the Business details tab.
  3. Change the phone number, email or address.
  4. Click Save settings at the top right.

The phone number may have two boxes: one for how it looks, and one for the number the Call button dials. If you change the number, change both.

The other tabs hold other parts that repeat across the site, for example the menu, the footer at the bottom of every page, and the buttons. [[One plain line on any other tab this site has, or delete this sentence.]]

5. Photos

Change a photo on a page

  1. Open the page (see section 3) and find the photo box.
  2. Point at the photo and click the pencil to choose a different one, or the cross to remove it.
  3. Pick a photo from your library, or click Upload files to add a new one from your computer.
  4. Save the page.

Good photos

  • Use JPG photos. Large photos look best at least 1600 pixels wide.
  • Use photos you own or have permission to use.

Describe every photo

Some visitors cannot see photos. Their device reads out a short description instead. Google reads it too.

  1. Click Media, then click the photo.
  2. In the Alternative Text box, write what the photo shows in a few plain words, for example "Our team outside the front door".
  3. The description saves by itself. Close the window.

6. Forms and enquiries

Where enquiries go

When someone fills in a form on the website, the enquiry is emailed to {{LEAD_TO}}[[, with copies to the other addresses that get it]]. The visitor then sees a thank-you page.

Change who receives them

  1. Click Contact in the menu, then click the form name.
  2. Click the Mail tab.
  3. Change the email address in the To box. Leave the other boxes as they are.
  4. Click Save.

See past enquiries

Every enquiry is also saved on the website, in case an email goes missing. Click [[the enquiries menu name, for example Contact Forms]] in the menu, then the form name. [[If the Editor login cannot see this menu, write instead: Ask us if you want to see past enquiries here.]]

Enquiries hold people's personal details. Only give logins to people who should see them.

Spam

The forms block most spam by themselves. If a lot of spam gets through, tell us.
